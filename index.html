The Problem — Mastung Is Running Out of Water

Mastung is facing a slow, silent water crisis. It is not one dry year. It is a system that is extracting more water than nature can replace, year after year.

For generations, Mastung farmers relied on karez — underground gravity channels that delivered water without pumps and with almost no evaporation. In the last few decades, diesel and solar tube wells allowed much deeper and faster pumping. Wells now compete with each other and with the old karez tunnels. The water table has fallen below the level of many karez galleries, so karez that once flowed year-round now run dry.

At the same time, farmers have continued to grow water-intensive crops, season after season, on the same land. Continuous wheat, with high irrigation demand and long growing periods, places the greatest pressure on an already falling aquifer — especially in the hot, dry months of April to July, when rainfall is almost zero and temperatures exceed 26°C.

Why Is This Happening? — Root Causes

Over-pumping by Tube Wells
Extraction is faster than natural recharge. Every new well lowers the water table for neighbours and for karez users.
Loss of Karez Discipline
Karez delivered limited, shared water. Tube wells removed that limit, so water is no longer used as a common, protected resource.
Water-Heavy Cropping
Continuous wheat and other thirsty crops demand irrigation even when rain fails and soil is already dry.
Hot, Dry Climate
High temperature + high evapotranspiration + rainfall under 0.1 mm/day in Apr–Jul 2025 = soil loses water faster than it gains it.
High Evaporation Loss
Open channels and flood irrigation lose water to sun and wind. Karez avoided this by keeping water underground.
No Rest for the Land
Land is rarely left fallow. Without rest and legumes, soil structure weakens and needs even more water and fertilizer.

What Happens If We Do Nothing?

Karez Will Keep Drying
More tunnels will fall below the water table and stop flowing permanently.
Wells Will Cost More
Deeper drilling, more diesel / solar cost, lower discharge — small farmers suffer first.
Soil Will Degrade
Dry, tired soil holds less moisture, needs more fertilizer, and yields less.
Incomes Will Fall
Crop failure in dry years will increase. Farmers will depend on one crop, one season.
Salinity Will Increase
As water falls and evaporation rises, salts remain in the topsoil, making land harder to farm.
Migration Will Increase
When farming no longer pays, families leave. Protecting water is protecting the village.

Timeline — How the Crisis Developed

Before — Karez Era: Gravity-fed, shared water. Recharge balanced extraction. Water table stable.
Transition — Tube Well Era: Pumping increased rapidly. Water table began falling 3–4 ft per year.
2021 — Extreme Drought: Only 0.17 mm/day rainfall. Soil moisture collapsed.
2022 — Brief Recovery: Soil wetness 0.54 — the wettest year. A chance to save water was missed.
2024 — Wet Year: 1.05 mm/day rainfall. Good harvest, but pumping did not reduce.
2025 — Crisis: Annual wetness 0.26, below the 0.3 deficit line. Apr–Jul: 0.13–0.14, rain under 0.1 mm/day, temperature above 26°C.

Conclusion: The problem is not lack of knowledge. It is lack of a water-first choice at planting time. This tool provides that choice.

Karez — Centuries of Local Knowledge

Karez is a gently sloping underground tunnel with vertical shafts. It taps groundwater and delivers it by gravity — no pump, almost no evaporation loss.

"Karez systems delivered water by gravity with almost no evaporation loss. When tube wells replaced them, groundwater fell faster than recharge. Rotation choices that reduce water demand help protect what remains — for karez users and well users alike."

Scientific Basis

  • Soil moisture anomaly: Current minus long-term average. Negative = water deficit.
  • NDVI: Satellite greenness. Declining NDVI during season signals crop stress.
  • Evapotranspiration: Water lost from soil + plants. High ET + low rain = groundwater drawdown.
  • Resolution: GPM (0.1°) and SMAP (9 km) resampled to 1 km via bilinear interpolation.
  • Karez context: Gravity-fed underground channels historically sustained Mastung without pumping. Tube wells lowered the water table below karez tunnels.
